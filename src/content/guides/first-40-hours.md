Most beginner guides are organised by topic: here's the class system, here's professions, here's
camping. That's the wrong order — you don't choose professions the moment you log in, and by the time
the guide reaches camping you've already missed twenty levels of free buffs.

This one is ordered by **when you actually have to decide something**, from the week before launch to
the point where your first character is done.

## Before launch: three things worth deciding early

**1. What you're playing.** Not because the choice is irreversible — it isn't — but because Forever
rewrote the class kits enough that what a class *used* to be is a poor guide to what it is now.

There are **56 legal race and class combinations**, and **six of them never existed in Classic**:
Human Hunter, Dwarf Shaman, Gnome Priest, Orc Mage, Troll Warlock and Undead Paladin. The
[race × class matrix](/race-class/) has all 56 with links, and the [class pages](/classes/) show the
three talent trees each one actually ships.

If you have beta access, spend an hour on two or three classes you're considering. Finding out at
level 40 that you don't like a class is worse than finding out in week one.

**2. What profession.** Two you can train literally at level 1 — the starting zones have profession
trainers and node spawns now, so there's no trip to a capital city. See
[professions](/professions/) for every recipe each one ships.

**3. Reserve a name** between **October 27 and November 3**. Included with any upgrade purchase, up to
three characters. Names are first-come.

## Level 1–6: the starting zone does more than it used to

Two changes worth knowing before you sprint out:

- **Gathering trains in the starting zone.** Mining, herbalism and skinning are all learnable where you
  spawn, and the nodes are there to match. The old run-to-the-city ritual is gone.
- **Exploration pays experience.** Following the side path on the way out of the starting zone is worth
  roughly a level by itself — one walkthrough leaves at **level 6** instead of 5, which is the
  difference between training your level-6 spells or not.

**Do this now:** train two professions. If you're planning to make gold, that means two gathering
professions; if you want to be self-sufficient, one gathering and one crafting. Herbalism pairs with
Alchemy and First Aid; Mining pairs with Blacksmithing and Engineering.

## Level 5: camping unlocks, and it's not optional

A quest called **The Great Outdoors** appears around level 5. It sends you to town to train Cooking and
build your first campfire.

This is the single most-skipped system by new players, and it's free stats. Camp buffs last **one
hour** and stack with everything you already have:

| Object | What you get |
|---|---|
| Camp Tent (Leatherworking) | One bar of rested experience |
| Sharpening Wheel (Blacksmithing) | Strength — costs a Rough Stone and a Copper Bar |
| Fishbowl (Fishing) | **+8% to stats**, the biggest single number on the list |

Full table with all twelve professions in the [camping guide](/guides/camping/).

**The hourly routine:** find or start a camp → sit for **one minute** → collect every buff stack →
quest for an hour → rebuild. Sixty seconds of sitting buys an hour of stats.

One structural note: **you can only place one camp feature yourself.** The rest come from other
players. That's deliberate — a camp with five objects is a camp five professions built.

## Level 15–40: dungeons stop being skippable

This is the change that catches Classic veterans hardest.

**Dungeon mob kills now pay close to nothing. Dungeon quests pay enormously** — the reported ceiling is
**up to two levels per dungeon**. Two beta reports, both consistent: a warrior gained two levels from a
single Ruins of Lordaeron quest run, and a Horde character went **14 to 20** across Ragefire Chasm,
Ruins of Lordaeron and Wailing Caverns and called that the whole route.

In Classic, speed levelers skipped dungeons because assembling a group cost more than the instance paid
back. That's inverted. [Dungeon levels and quest locations →](/guides/dungeons/)

**The consequence for your play:** you'll cover fewer zones than a Classic route, and group
availability matters more than route optimisation. Tanks and healers are the bottleneck.

## Level 25: Legacy opens

An account-wide progression track unlocks when your first character hits **25** — or when you reach 150
skill in a non-gathering profession, or explore the whole map.

The number that matters: your account can earn **65 points**, but **each character can only spend 16**.
A single character can earn **29** without alts, so you will always be leaving something on the table.

Pick two trees and commit. Spreading 16 points across all three gets you nothing at a useful rank in
any of them. [Full Legacy breakdown — all 21 perks →](/guides/legacy-system/)

Two perks worth planning around: **Talented** unlocks talent points five levels early, and **Well
Rested** raises the rested cap. Both compound over a whole 1–60 run.

## Every level: what 1–60 actually costs

**4,084,700 experience**, from the client's own level curve. With a full buff stack that halves:

| | XP you have to actually earn |
|---|---|
| No buffs | 4,084,700 |
| +100% (Discoverer's Delight + Variety Show) | 2,042,350 |

Buffs **add, they don't multiply** — two +50% buffs give +100%, not +125%. Work out your own stack with
the [XP calculator](/tools/xp-calculator/). [The full leveling breakdown →](/guides/leveling/)

## The five mistakes the beta keeps repeating

These come up in every beginner walkthrough, which is a good sign they're easy to make:

1. **Rushing past quest text.** The quest log tells you where things are. When a quest says the target
   is underground, that means find a cave — and no addon will tell you that.
2. **Buying gear you'll replace next level.** Training costs climb sharply. A green bow off the auction
   house is competing with your spell ranks, and the bow loses. Check whether a dungeon or quest drops
   it first.
3. **Treating professions as optional.** They gate camp objects, which gate group buffs. A character
   with no profession contributes nothing to a camp.
4. **Skipping dungeons.** Covered above — it's the largest single change to leveling in Forever.
5. **Ignoring First Aid.** Bandages come from the cloth humanoids drop while you quest anyway, and
   they're the cheapest healing you'll have. Train it early and it levels itself.

- [ ] Decide a class and a race — the [matrix](/race-class/) has all 56 combinations
- [ ] Reserve a name, October 27 to November 3
- [ ] Pick two professions you'll actually level
- [ ] Know where your first campfire quest is — level 5, "The Great Outdoors"

## What this guide can't tell you

**Quest content.** Quest text and rewards live on Blizzard's servers, not in the client, so a detailed
1–60 route can't be built from data — it has to come from playing. The
[leveling guide](/guides/leveling/) covers the structure and the maths; the step-by-step route is
something creators are publishing video by video.

**Exact dungeon quest payouts.** The "two levels per dungeon" figure is field reporting from the beta.
It will shift between level bands and may be tuned before launch.

Every number on this page that isn't attributed to a creator came out of the client for build
1.60.1.70009, and gets rebuilt when that changes.
